01 — Profile

A practitioner first, an academic by craft.

I earned my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering (Bioengineering specialization) from Florida Atlantic University, where my doctoral work centered on the design of impedance-based biosensor electronics, embedded readout instrumentation, and computational imaging for point-of-care viral diagnostics. Since then, I have spent the last several years inside FDA-regulated medical-device industry — building Class II diagnostic instruments end-to-end, leading assay integration on clinical chemistry platforms, and running design controls under ISO 13485 and 21 CFR 820.

I work where electronics, biology, and signal analysis meet. The same circuit that detects a viral antigen in 10 μL of blood is, at heart, an applied Electrical Engineering problem: front-end conditioning, analog-to-digital conversion, embedded control, and the inference layer that turns a voltage into a clinical answer. Teaching that pipeline — from a freshman’s first op-amp to a senior’s capstone instrument — is where I want to spend the rest of my career.

Before industry, I taught nine undergraduate Electrical & Electronic Engineering courses at Uttara University in Bangladesh, served as Dean’s Teaching Assistant and instructor of record for the Control Systems Laboratory at Florida Atlantic University, founded an IEEE Student Branch, and mentored graduate and undergraduate researchers whose work led to peer-reviewed publications.

02 — Research

Four threads, one practice.

My published work and ongoing interests cluster around four interconnected areas of Electrical Engineering applied to diagnostics and healthcare instrumentation.

01

Biosensor Electronics & Impedance Spectroscopy

Design and characterization of electrochemical impedance sensors on paper, PMMA, and flexible substrates. Frequency-domain signal analysis across clinically relevant analyte ranges for viral and protein detection.

02

Embedded Instrumentation for Point-of-Care

Analog signal conditioning, ADC interfaces, embedded readout platforms, and closed-loop control electronics (magnetic actuation, LAMP heating) for automated microfluidic diagnostic cartridges.

03

Computational Imaging & Signal Analysis

Replacing conventional optical microscopy with CMOS detector arrays; applying deep learning to signal reconstruction; smartphone-readable color quantification for assay outputs.

04

Engineering Education & Curriculum Research

ABET-aligned laboratory modernization, MATLAB/Simulink-to-hardware integrated pedagogy, and outcome-based assessment design for undergraduate Electrical Engineering instruction.

04 — Teaching

Twelve courses, three countries, one philosophy.

Engineers learn best when they touch the thing they are studying. Every course I have designed has put a soldering iron, a MATLAB script, an oscilloscope, or a piece of fabricated hardware in students’ hands — because Electrical Engineering is a discipline of physical objects, not paper abstractions.

05 — Industry

Three years inside the regulated medical-device industry.

FDA Class II device development end-to-end, statistical signal analysis on a clinical chemistry platform, and assay manufacturing under ISO 13485. Industry depth that returns to the classroom as case material.

2024 — Present

Senior Development Scientist · Chaska, MN

Cross-functional assay integration onto the DxC500i clinical chemistry platform under FDA 21 CFR 820 design controls. Applied JMP-based statistical signal analysis (SPC, DOE) to resolve drift, carryover, and calibration instability — delivering a documented 40% revenue improvement on the affected product line.

2023 — 2024

Ethos Biosciences

Senior Scientist · Logan Township, NJ

Led lateral-flow assay manufacturing process development under ISO 13485. Achieved a 65% production-cost reduction through in-house nanoparticle conjugation optimization and process scale-up. Developed validated analytical measurement methods for in-process QC and release testing.

2021 — 2023

In Vitro Diagnostic Solutions (IVDS)

R&D Scientist · Cherry Hill, NJ

Full lifecycle ownership of an FDA Class II IVD — vertical-flow G6PD assay and handheld POC reflectance reader — from feasibility through prototype (CAD, 3D printing, laser cutting) and clinical validation with the University of Oxford. Secured $190K+ in competitive external funding and built the ISO 9001/13485 quality system from inception.

These years also yielded the regulatory and quality fluency that strengthens classroom delivery: design controls, V&V, CAPA, statistical process control, and the lived experience of taking a circuit from bench to clinic.
